Volunteer Tracker Help

Plan the shifts you need people for, record the hours they work, and produce a verification letter a court or a school will accept. This help covers both halves and the point where they meet.

New here? Start with Set up the plugin. Running a copy of your site? Read Stop a copied site emailing volunteers first — it's the one mistake that reaches real people.

Get started

What the Volunteer Tracker does

It does two jobs that are really one job.

Forwards. Plan the shifts you need people for, let them sign up, remind them, and print the roster for the clipboard.

Backwards. Record the hours that were actually worked, have a staff member attest to them, and produce a verification letter.

The two halves meet when a shift ends. The roster becomes the hours, with everybody who signed up already listed and selected and the scheduled times filled in. Clear the checkbox for whoever didn't come, add the walk-ins, and save once. You never type Saturday twice.

What the letter doesn't claim

The letter reports what your organization recorded. It doesn't certify anything, because nobody at Groundwork Common watched anybody sweep a floor. There's no seal, no rendered signature, and no affidavit language — the signature block is a ruled line for a real person to sign.

You can reword the disclaimer if your counsel wants different wording. You can't empty it.

Set up the plugin

Five things, once. Allow about twenty minutes.

1. Tell the letter who you are

  1. Go to Volunteer Hours›Settings›Letter.
  2. Fill in Organization name, Address, Contact for questions, and Signed by.
  3. Select Save.
Important

Each of these falls back to something reasonable on its own — the site title, your administrator's email address, an unlabeled signature line. Together, those fallbacks produce a letter headed with your website's title, over your webmaster's address, signed by nobody.

Make the contact one that's answered. It's the number somebody calls to check a reference code.

2. Choose who can verify and who can issue

Go to Settings›Permissions. See Choose who can verify and issue.

3. Decide how long you keep records

Go to Settings›Privacy and save the tab, even if you keep the default. A notice stays on the plugin's screens until you do. See Choose how long to keep records.

4. Try it once on somebody made up

See Try it once before you rely on it.

5. Turn on only what you need

Everything public is off on a new install:

  • Settings›Shifts›Plan shifts ahead of time adds the Schedule screen.
  • Settings›Shifts›Let people sign up for shifts themselves is a separate switch, and needs a page.
  • Settings›Logging›Let volunteers send in their own hours adds the public hours form, and needs a page.
Note

If you take signups on the phone and a clipboard, you can leave all three off. Nothing else depends on them.

Choose who can verify and issue

Anybody who can edit posts on your site can log hours and see volunteer records. Two decisions are held separately, because they're bigger.

PermissionLets somebody
Verify hoursAttest, as a staff member, that a shift was worked.
Issue lettersPut your organization's name on a document a court reads.
  1. Go to Volunteer Hours›Settings›Permissions.
  2. Select the roles that can verify hours, and the roles that can issue letters.
  3. Select Save.

Administrators and Editors hold both by default.

Note

These are separate on purpose. The coordinator who knows Jane swept the warehouse and the director who signs a letter to a probation officer are often different people.

A role with neither still sees the hours list and can type up a shift. It has no Verify button and no Letters screen, rather than buttons that refuse it.

Tip

Anyone with either permission can check a reference code somebody has called about. Only Issue letters can produce one.

Try it once before you rely on it

Do the whole loop on a made-up person. It takes five minutes and it's the fastest way to find a setting you missed.

  1. Go to Volunteer Hours›Volunteers and add a volunteer called Test Person.
  2. Go to Log hours and record four hours for them.
  3. Open the entry and select Verify these hours.
  4. Go to Letters, choose Test Person, and select Preview.
  5. Select Open the letter to print and read it. Check the letterhead, the contact, and the signature line.
  6. Delete the entry and the volunteer record.

Find what needs doing

Go to Volunteer Hours›Dashboard. It shows two things: what's waiting for you, and how to get everywhere else.

Needs you is a worklist. Everything on it is a job, and doing the job makes the line disappear. It's ordered by what you lose if it waits, not by which number is biggest:

  1. A shift that happened and was never typed up. Every week takes those hours further from anybody's memory.
  2. A shift this week that's short of people. On Sunday there's nothing to be done about Saturday.
  3. Hours awaiting verification, and website submissions awaiting matching. Both keep.
  4. Somebody past their deadline.

A queue with nothing in it doesn't appear at all.

Note

Nobody is named on this screen. Every line is a count and a link, and the names are on the screen the link goes to.

This year reports verified hours only — the figure your Form 990 and your grant reports want. Hours still awaiting verification are counted beside it rather than folded in. The year runs from 1 January; a developer can change that with the gwc_vt_reporting_year_start filter.

Coming up runs two weeks, split into this week and next.

Volunteers

Add a volunteer

A volunteer record is a record of a person. It isn't a WordPress account — nobody signs in — and none of it is public or searchable.

  1. Go to Volunteer Hours›Volunteers›Add volunteer.
  2. Enter their name as the title.
  3. Add an Email if you have one. It's where a verification letter goes, and how the privacy tools find this record.
  4. Add a Phone if you want one. It's for calling around when a shift is short, and it never appears on a letter or on your website.
  5. Select Publish.

The volunteer list shows verified hours, hours awaiting verification, anything still required, and the date of their last shift. You can filter it to people who have hours to complete, people past their deadline, or people with no requirement.

Track required hours and a deadline

Use these for somebody doing court-ordered or school-required service.

  1. Open the volunteer's record.
  2. Enter Hours they have to complete.
  3. Enter By when, if there's a deadline. It shows as a countdown on their record and in the volunteer list.
  4. Enter Who requires it, so you know which program they're here under.
  5. Select Update.
Important

None of this ever appears on a letter, and there's no setting that puts it there. How many hours a court ordered is a fact about the court's own document, which you may have seen as a photograph and may be reading wrong. Printing “120 ordered, 94 completed” would be your organization certifying the terms of an order back to the court that issued it.

Only verified hours count toward a requirement. Unverified hours are reported next to the figure rather than added in, so somebody four hours short with six unverified is a ten-second problem instead of an invisible one.

There's no “on track” indicator. Overdue means the date has passed and the hours aren't there.

Keep a record longer than your policy

Use a hold when a court requires you to keep something longer than your own retention policy allows.

  1. Open the volunteer's record.
  2. Select Keep this record regardless of the retention policy.
  3. Enter a reason in Why it is held.
  4. Select Update.
Note

A hold also blocks an erasure request from WordPress's privacy tools. Whoever handles that request sees the reason you typed, so they can explain the refusal.

Hours

Four ways hours get recorded

An hour entry is one occasion of work: who, when, how long, doing what, supervised by whom. Pick whichever route matches how the information reached you.

When you haveUse
One person, one shiftLog hours
A marked-up sign-in sheetLog a day
A shift people signed up forTurn a roster into hours
Something a volunteer sent from your websiteMatch hours sent from your website

However they arrive, hours start out unverified and stay that way until a staff member attests to them.

Type hours the way you say them

Wherever you enter a duration, all of these record three and a half hours:

3.5      3:30      3h 30m      210m
Important

A bare number means hours. So 210 means two hundred and ten hours, which is nobody's shift — it's almost always minutes typed into an hours field. Anything longer than a single day is refused rather than guessed at. Write 210m instead.

Rounding is always to the nearest increment, never up. Set the increment at Settings›Logging›Round hours to. The default is 15 minutes, and the screen tells you when it has rounded.

Rounding up would mean crediting hours nobody worked, which on this document is the one direction of error that matters.

Nothing is corrected silently. Unreadable hours, a date in the future, or a volunteer that couldn't be attached each say so on screen. These are the numbers a letter prints.

Note

Dates in the future are refused by default, because a shift dated next Tuesday is a typo far more often than a plan. If you need them, turn on Settings›Logging›Allow shifts dated in the future.

Log hours for one person

  1. Go to Volunteer Hours›Log hours.
  2. Search for the volunteer and select them. If they're not on file, select Add the volunteer first.
  3. Set Date of the shift.
  4. Enter Hours worked. See Type hours the way you say them.
  5. Enter What they did. This appears on the verification letter, so write it the way you'd want a probation officer to read it.
  6. Enter Supervised by — the person who was there, who isn't necessarily whoever verifies the entry.
  7. Select Publish.

To verify it now, see Verify hours.

Log a whole sign-in sheet

Use this when you're typing up a paper sheet from a day everybody worked together.

  1. Go to Volunteer Hours›Log a day.
  2. Under The shift, set the date, what they did, and who supervised. Your name is prefilled as the supervisor — change it if it was somebody else.
  3. Under Who worked it, choose a volunteer and enter their hours on each row.
  4. Select Add more rows if you need them. Leave a row empty to skip it.
  5. Select Log the hours for this shift.

Every entry arrives unverified, the same as any other.

Match hours sent from your website

Hours submitted through the public form arrive attached to nobody. The form never looks anybody up, so the name and email on it are claims, not an identity. A staff member decides whose they are.

  1. On the dashboard, select Match them. Or filter All hours to Awaiting matching.
  2. Open an entry and read the submitted name and email at the top.
  3. Select Attach to the suggested volunteer, choose a different one from the list, or select Create a volunteer from this.
  4. Select Update.
Note

Check the claim before you attach it. It's whatever the person typed, and nothing has confirmed it.

Matching isn't verifying. The entry still needs a staff member to attest to it. See Verify hours.

Verify and issue

Verify hours

Verifying records that you, as a staff member, attest these hours were worked. Your name and the date are recorded, and both appear on the verification letter beside the shift.

You need the Verify hours permission.

Verify one entry

  1. Open the hour entry. If you've just created it, save it first.
  2. Select Verify these hours.

Work through the queue

  1. Go to Volunteer Hours›All hours.
  2. Set Filter by verification to Awaiting verification.
  3. Open each entry and verify it.
Important

Only verified hours count. They're the only ones your organization stands behind, the only ones counted toward a requirement, and the only ones a letter states a total for.

Note

Turning a roster into hours isn't verifying. Matching says whose hours these are, logging says the shift has been written up, and verifying says it happened. Three questions, three answers, often three people.

Withdraw a verification

  1. Open the hour entry.
  2. Select Withdraw verification.
  3. Read what it tells you, then confirm.

The hours themselves are untouched. They stop counting toward anything, and stop appearing as verified on a letter, until somebody attests to them again.

Note

Verifying again afterwards doesn't restore the original attestation. It records whoever does it, on the day they do it.

Create a verification letter

You need the Issue letters permission.

  1. Go to Volunteer Hours›Letters.
  2. Under Produce a letter, choose the volunteer.
  3. Set From and To. Leave both empty to cover their whole time volunteering with you.
  4. Select Preview and check the verified total, the number of shifts, the period, and the reference code.
  5. Select Open the letter to print, or Email it.
Note

Both actions are logged, printing included. A printed letter has left the building just as much as an emailed one.

If none of the hours has been verified yet, the preview says so and refuses. A letter reporting zero verified hours isn't something to send.

To email it, the volunteer needs an email address on their record.

What's on the letter

  • Your letterhead: logo, organization name, and address.
  • An opening paragraph naming the volunteer, the verified hours, and the period.
  • An itemized table: date, hours, activity, supervisor, and who attested to it and when.
  • The verified total and the number of shifts.
  • The disclaimer.
  • A reference code, and a note saying what it proves.
  • A ruled signature line, with the signatory's name and title underneath.

Optionally it also lists hours nobody has verified, marked as such and explicitly excluded from the total. That's off by default, and they're never quietly added in.

Tip

The letter is styled for print. To make a file, use your browser's Print to PDF.

Change the letter wording

  1. Go to Volunteer Hours›Settings›Letter.
  2. Edit the opening paragraph, the disclaimer, the reference note, the email subject, or the covering note.
  3. Select Save.

These placeholders are filled in for each volunteer:

{org}   {name}    {hours}     {shifts}  {period}
{contact}  {reference}  {timestamp}  {timezone}
Important

Two fields can't be emptied: the Disclaimer and What the reference code proves. Saving either one blank restores the built-in wording. They're what tell a reader that your organization, not this plugin, is the authoritative record-keeper.

Note

The email's covering note sits outside the letter. The emailed letter and the printed letter are the same document — a short “here's your letter” message is added around it, not inside it.

Use a From address at your own domain. A letter sent from an address somewhere else is the one a spam filter is most likely to hold back, and the volunteer never learns it was sent.

Check a reference code

Somebody calls: “I'm holding a letter from you, reference FBM-42-20260214-9C4F2A17.” Here's how to answer them.

You need either permission — Verify hours or Issue letters.

  1. Go to Volunteer Hours›Letters.
  2. Under Check a reference, type the code.
  3. Select Check it.
What it saysWhat that means
No letter with that reference has been issued from this siteExactly that.
This letter matches our current recordsThe document they're holding agrees with what you have now, field for field.
Our records have changed sinceSomething has been corrected, verified, or withdrawn since the letter went out. Not necessarily anybody's fault.

The screen then renders the whole letter, rebuilt from your records as they stand right now. Read it down the phone against theirs — the code can tell you that something differs, and only the document shows what.

Note

This is generated live, not from a stored copy. The plugin doesn't keep one. Checking a reference never logs an issuance.

The code covers every field the letter prints, not just the total — so a swapped date, a rewritten activity, or a changed supervisor all change it.

A code also carries the date the letter was produced, so two letters for the same volunteer and the same period, produced on different days, have different codes. Both are checkable.

Important

Reference codes don't survive a change of your WordPress security salts. That's deliberate — it's what stops one being forged without access to your database. If you rotate salts, existing codes stop checking.

Schedule

Turn on the schedule

  1. Go to Volunteer Hours›Settings›Shifts.
  2. Select Plan shifts ahead of time.
  3. Select Save.

A Schedule screen appears in the menu.

Important

A shift is a plan, not a record. Nobody accrues hours by being on a Saturday and not turning up. Hours are logged afterwards, by somebody who was there, and they still have to be verified.

Letting people sign up from your website is a second switch. See Let people sign up for shifts.

  2. Fill in the fields below.
  3. Select Save.

Repeat a shift

  1. While adding or editing a shift, set Repeat.
  2. Set the until date.
  3. Select Save.

Your choices are: just this once, every day, every weekday, every week, every other week, or every month on the same weekday.

Every occurrence becomes a real shift that you can edit or cancel on its own. Closing the Saturday after Thanksgiving doesn't disturb the rest of the season.

Note

You can create up to 200 shifts at a time, and up to 12 months ahead. If your request would go past either limit, the screen tells you how many it made. It doesn't quietly make fewer.

Manage who is coming

Open a shift from the schedule to see its roster.

Put somebody on a shift

  1. Under Put somebody on this shift, choose a volunteer.
  2. Select Add.

They need a volunteer record first. Staff add people by picking a record rather than typing a name and address, because a typed one would create an unmatched claim — which is the public form's job, not yours.

Take somebody off

Select Take off beside their name. This records that they withdrew rather than deleting them, so a glance at the shift shows that two people dropped instead of leaving you wondering. Whoever is next on the waiting list takes the free place right away.

Print the roster

Select Print the roster. You get the sheet for the clipboard: contact details, blank columns for in and out times and initials, and six spare rows for people who turn up without having signed up.

Tip

Bring the sheet back marked up and use Turn a roster into hours. Everybody who signed up is already listed and selected.

Waiting lists

The maximum on a shift isn't a limit on who may sign up. Once it's reached, later signups go on a waiting list instead of being turned away, and you decide what to do with them.

That's deliberate. Somebody working off a court order with a deadline shouldn't be bounced by a number a coordinator typed in March. If you can see the list, you can have a conversation; a refusal is a dead end the volunteer can't appeal.

  • When somebody withdraws, the next person on the waiting list takes the place automatically.
  • You can promote somebody by hand from the shift's roster.
  • Lowering a shift's maximum makes the shift smaller. It doesn't un-invite the people already coming.
  • Nobody on the waiting list gets a reminder. They don't have a place, and telling them to turn up would be telling them something untrue.

Cancel or delete a shift

These are different things.

ActionWhat it saysWhen you can use it
CancelIt was called off, and everybody can see that it was. The shift stays on the schedule, struck through, with your reason.Any time.
DeleteIt never existed, which is only true of a typo.Only while nobody is signed up.
  1. Open the shift.
  2. Under Calling it off, enter Why it was canceled.
  3. Choose whether to email everybody on the roster. You're shown the count first.
  4. Select Call it off.
Note

Emailing the roster is always a choice, never a side effect — and only a move offers it. Changing the date, the times, the overnight setting, or the address counts as a move. Rewording the activity, correcting the supervisor, expanding the notes, or changing the capacity emails nobody.

Turn a roster into hours

Do this after the shift has ended. Everybody who signed up is already listed and selected, with the date, activity, and supervisor filled in from the shift.

  1. Go to Volunteer Hours›Schedule.
  2. Find the shift under Already happened and select Log the hours.
  3. Under Who turned up, clear Came for anybody who didn't come.
  4. Adjust anybody's hours if the day ran differently.
  5. Use the blank rows at the bottom for people who came without signing up.
  6. Select Log the hours for this shift.
  • You can't log a shift before it ends. Logging Saturday on Friday would date Saturday's hours to Friday, on the document a court reads.
  • Nothing records that somebody didn't turn up. A no-show is worked out from a logged shift, a place on the roster, and no entry — never stored as a flag.
  • You can come back. Reopen a logged shift to add the walk-in you forgot. Anybody who already has an entry is shown without a checkbox and can't be logged twice.
  • The entries arrive unverified. See Verify hours.

Events

Plan an event

An event is one occasion with several roles offered at several times — a festival, a meal service, a collection drive.

Underneath, there's nothing new. Every time on an event is an ordinary shift, so waiting lists, reminders, rosters, calendar files, cancellations, and hours all work exactly as they do for a shift you scheduled on its own.

  1. Go to Volunteer Hours›Schedule and select Add an event.
  2. Enter a Name volunteers will recognize — “Fall Festival”, not “Event 3”.
  3. Enter What it is. It appears above the times on the event's page.
  4. Enter Where and Supervisor for the whole event.
  5. Under Who you need, name a role, then add its times underneath.
  6. Select + Add another role for each further role.
  7. Select Save.
Note

You don't set the event's date. It's read from the times in the grid, because a second date field is a second answer, and it disagrees with the first the moment somebody moves a time.

The most specific value wins, and nothing is added together. A time's own supervisor beats the role's, which beats the event's. For a genuine one-off note, open that single time in the shift editor.

Important

Role names are printed on volunteers' verification letters. Write them accordingly.

Put an event on a page

An event has no web address of its own, and publishing it doesn't give it one. It's seen only on a page you put it on.

  1. Edit or create the page you want it on.
  2. Add the Volunteer Event block and choose the event. Or paste the shortcode with the event's ID: 
    [gwc_vt_event_grid id="123"]
  3. Publish the page.

The event editor tells you which page currently shows it, and says so plainly when no page does.

Three things have to be true before anybody can sign up

  1. Signing up from your website is turned on, at Settings›Shifts.
  2. A shifts page is pinned at Settings›Shifts. You need this even though the event sits on its own page, because every public signup goes through it.
  3. The event is published.
Note

An event's times never appear on the general shifts page. That page lists shifts you scheduled on their own; an event is shown whole, on its own page, or not at all.

Visitors see each role, each time, and how many places are left — never who else is coming.

Print the event roster

Open the event and select Print the roster. You get one sheet, split by role and time, with the same in, out, and initials columns. The roster screen also flags anybody down for two times that overlap. Nothing is blocked — ducking out of the kitchen at noon to greet is a real thing people do — but you're told.

Call off a time or drop a role

Each of these is a button with its own confirmation screen, not something you do by clearing a field.

Call off one time

  1. Open the event and select Call off on the row.
  2. Enter Why. It shows on the schedule, and in the email if you send one.
  3. Choose whether to email everybody on it.
  4. Select Call it off.

Drop a whole role

  1. Open the event and select Remove this whole role.
  2. Read what will happen. Times with people on them are called off and kept; times nobody is on are deleted.
  3. Enter Why, and choose whether to email anybody affected.
  4. Select Drop the role.
Note

Removing a time cancels it when people are on it and deletes it only when nobody is. The row tells you which before you save.

A canceled time is struck through, carries its reason, can't be edited, and offers only the action that makes sense — putting it back on.

Copy an event to a new date

  1. Open the event.
  2. Select Copy.
  3. Change the dates on the new copy.
  4. Publish it when you're happy with it.

The copy includes every role and time, and it's saved as a draft so you can check the dates before anybody sees it.

Note

Events are copied rather than repeated. A repeating grid would need a rule per role per time, plus an exception list per occurrence, and a signup would have to attach to something that isn't a real row.

Your website

Let people sign up for shifts

  1. Create a page and add the Volunteer Shifts block, or the [gwc_vt_shift_list] shortcode. Publish it.
  2. Go to Volunteer Hours›Settings›Shifts.
  3. Select Let people sign up for shifts themselves.
  4. Choose your page under The page the shifts are on.
  5. Select Save.

Visitors see what each shift is, when and where, what to know, and how many places are left. They give a name and an email address. No account is created and nobody signs in.

Important

The list never shows who is coming, and no setting changes that. On a site running a court-ordered service program, a roster is a list of people working one off. A place count says nothing about anybody; a first name says everything about one person.

Tune the list

SettingWhat it doesDefault
Show shifts up toHow far ahead the public list looks.2 months ahead

Note

The code isn't a security control. It's the difference between a form the whole internet can post to and one only people who were told about it will bother with.

Somebody who signs up isn't added to your volunteer records automatically. A staff member matches them, the same as with the hours form.

Let volunteers send in hours

  1. Create a page and add the Volunteer Hours Form block, or the [gwc_vt_hours_form] shortcode. Publish it.
  2. Go to Volunteer Hours›Settings›Logging.
  3. Select Let volunteers send in their own hours.
  4. Choose your page under The page the form is on.
  5. Select Save.

Visitors give a name, an optional email, the date, how long they worked, what they did, and who supervised them. You can require a front-desk code here too.

Everything sent arrives unverified and attached to nobody, and waits for a staff member. Nothing anybody enters appears publicly. See Match hours sent from your website.

Note

The form never looks anybody up. No part of it behaves differently depending on whether the email matches an existing volunteer — which is what stops the form becoming a way to ask whether a named person is working off a court order.

For the same reason, an accepted submission, one that tripped the spam trap, and one that hit the rate limit all return exactly the same message.

Blocks and shortcodes

Three blocks, each with a shortcode that does the same thing. Use whichever suits the page.

BlockShortcodeShows

Volunteer Event[gwc_vt_event_grid id="123"]One event, all roles and times, and a form for picking several.

Important

For the shift list and the hours form, adding the block isn't enough. You also have to pin that page in Settings. Submissions are only accepted on the pinned page.

The page is pinned by ID, so renaming it changes nothing.

The confirmation isn't a setting. A signup with no confirmation leaves somebody with no record of what they agreed to, no note of where to go, and no way out except calling during office hours.

The confirmation and the reminder both carry a calendar link and a cancel link. The cancel link opens a page with a button on it — nothing is withdrawn by clicking a link, because mail clients prefetch links and security appliances follow them.

Note

Nobody is ever asked to confirm they're still coming. An unclicked cancel link means nothing, but an unclicked confirm link looks like an answer — and every way of reading that answer is bad. Promote from the waiting list by hand instead, on real information.

A shift that has already started is never reminded about.

Turn on shift reminders

  1. Go to Volunteer Hours›Settings›Shifts.
  2. Select Remind people before their shift.
  3. Set Send the reminder to the evening before, a day before, two days before, or three days before.
  4. Select Save.

Each person gets one message per shift, sent once. Two days is usually right: long enough that somebody who can't make it still has time to say so, and you still have time to call somebody else.

Note

People on the waiting list don't get a reminder, because they don't have a place yet.

Get a daily summary

  1. Go to Volunteer Hours›Settings›Shifts.
  2. Select Send yourself a daily summary.
  3. Enter an address in Send the summary to, or leave it empty to use your site's admin address.
  4. Select Save.

The summary lists shifts in the next week that are short of people, and shifts that have happened without their hours being logged.

Note

Nothing is sent on a day with nothing to report. A summary that arrived every morning saying “nothing to report” would get a filter rule inside two weeks — and then the one that says Saturday has two of six would get filtered with it.

Reminders and the daily summary are the only messages the plugin sends without somebody selecting a button, and both are off until you turn them on.

Stop a copied site emailing volunteers

Warning

This is the most important thing to know about running this plugin.

A copy of your site restored from a backup — a staging server, a developer's machine, a clone made to test an upgrade — has the real volunteers and the real email addresses in it, and a working cron. Left running, it sends reminders, confirmations, cancellation notices, and verification letters to those people, about court-ordered service, from a site nobody is watching.

Add one line to wp-config.php on the copy, before it runs:

define( 'GWC_VT_MAIL_MODE', 'off' );

That sends nothing at all.

To see what would have gone out instead:

define( 'GWC_VT_MAIL_MODE', 'trap' );
define( 'GWC_VT_MAIL_ALLOW', 'you@example.com' );

Every message then goes to that address, with the site's name in the subject and a line saying who it was really addressed to.

Note

Neither constant belongs on your live site. Unset means normal delivery, so production needs no configuration at all.

Trap mode with no valid address sends nothing, rather than falling through to the real recipient.

Records and privacy

Choose how long to keep records

Volunteer records here are more sensitive than most plugin data. In the mandated-service case they reveal that a named person is working off a court order. None of it is public, none of it is searchable, and none of it is exposed on the REST API.

  1. Go to Volunteer Hours›Settings›Privacy.
  2. Set Keep volunteer records for: indefinitely, 1 year, 18 months, 2, 3, 5, or 7 years.
  3. Set When a record is old enough to either remove the name and contact details and keep the hours, or delete the record and its shifts entirely.
  4. Set Measured from to their last recorded shift, or the last time a shift of theirs was verified.
  5. Select Save.
Tip

Anonymizing is usually right. Your grant reporting and your Form 990 need the service totals, and the hours identify nobody once the name is gone. Deleting outright throws away your own statistics to solve a problem that removing the identity already solved.

Note

Anonymizing removes the requirement along with the name. “120 hours required by 15 November for Franklin County Municipal Court” says a real person was under a real order, names it, and dates it — and it doesn't stop being a disclosure because the name above it has gone.

The clock runs on the whole person, not on each shift. A record is removed when the person has been inactive that long, and their old shifts go with them.

Measuring from the last verification is later, sometimes much later: a shift worked in March and attested in June counts from June.

Important

A notice stays on the plugin's screens until you save this tab — including saving it as “keep indefinitely”. That's a legitimate answer. Never having considered it isn't.

Export or erase someone's data

WordPress's own tools work with this plugin, whether or not you've set a retention period.

  1. Go to Tools›Export Personal Data or Tools›Erase Personal Data.
  2. Enter the person's email address.
  3. Follow WordPress's confirmation process.

Both cover volunteer records, logged shifts, issued letters, and shifts somebody signed up for — including a signup from a person who never became a volunteer, which nothing else in the plugin would find.

Important

The eraser names the letter references it affected. If a document a court is holding can no longer be checked, whoever handles the request has to know.

Note

The issued-letter log survives an erasure. A letter having been issued is a fact about your organization's own conduct, and the log holds a reference, an ID, and a date — no name.

A hold on a volunteer's record blocks an erasure request, and shows your reason to whoever handles it.

What happens if you delete the plugin

Nothing is removed. Every volunteer record, logged shift, scheduled shift, signup, and issued letter stays where it is, and so do the two permissions added to your roles. Deactivating does the same.

Important

That means deleting the plugin isn't a way to remove somebody's data. Use the retention policy or the Erase Personal Data tool first.

You can ask the plugin to clean up its own settings on deletion:

  1. Go to Volunteer Hours›Settings›Privacy.
  2. Under Removing this plugin, select the checkbox.
  3. Select Save.

Even then, it deletes no records of any kind — no posts, no post meta, and no capabilities.

Fix a problem

The hours came out wrong. I typed 210 for a three-and-a-half-hour shift.

210 means 210 hours, and it's refused rather than guessed at. Write 210m, 3.5, 3:30, or 3h 30m.

A letter shows fewer hours than I expected.

Only verified hours are totaled. Go to All hours, filter to Awaiting verification, and check that volunteer.

Somebody signed up, but their name isn't in Volunteers.

That's correct. A public signup is a claim, not an identity record. Match them to a volunteer record, or create one, when you're ready.

The reference check says our records have changed.

Something has been corrected, verified, or withdrawn since the letter went out. Read the letter rendered on that screen against the copy the caller is holding to see what differs.

Nothing appears on the page where I put the shift list.

Check three things: signups are turned on, this page is the one pinned at Settings›Shifts, and at least one upcoming shift is published.

My event's page is blank.

The event is probably still a draft. Also check that the block or shortcode names the right event ID, and that a shifts page is pinned.

No email is arriving.

Check wp-config.php for GWC_VT_MAIL_MODE. A copied site often has it, and it sometimes travels back to production by accident. Otherwise this is ordinary WordPress mail delivery, and an SMTP plugin is the usual fix.

Staff can't verify hours after a migration.

Check Settings›Permissions. A security plugin that rebuilds roles, or a restore from an old backup, can drop the two capabilities.

A supervisor wants to sign off without an account.

That isn't available in this version. A staff member with the Verify hours permission attests to hours, which is how the paper forms this replaces already work. Emailed supervisor confirmation is planned.
